The Doctrine of the Open Face

The Order does not wear its mask in public. It wears yours. A Weeper is the neighbor who brings soup, the colleague who covers your shift, the stranger who holds the door. When questioned, we do not resist. We do not hide. We grieve with you, and we mean it so well that you forget to ask what we are grieving for.

A tear that cannot be proven false is as good as a true one.

Unresolved Cases

  • FILE 0091 The Empty Ward [UNSOLVED] Eleven patients vanished from a locked wing between the 2 a.m. and 4 a.m. rounds. Every door was bolted from the inside. The night nurse was found knocked out in a corridor, a tear drawn on her cheek in what the lab called "an unidentified pigment." She has not spoken since.
  • FILE 0203 The ██████ Fire [UNSOLVED] An archive in ██████ burned to its foundation. One page survived, soaked in black ink, unreadable except for a few lines:
    "...he counted the stairs the night before..."
    "...we were kind to her. That is what she could not forgive..."
  • FILE 0412 The Unregistered Weapons [CLOSED / NO FINDINGS] Handgun recovered at scene. No serial number, no tool marks, no known manufacturer. Ballistics matched nothing on record. Case closed after the evidence locker was found empty and sealed from the outside; 2 guns were lost alongside a 500 bushwacker.
  • FILE 0438 Residence, Second Floor [CAUSE UNDETERMINED] Occupant reduced to ash. Furniture, curtains, and bedding untouched. No accelerant found. A gold-colored band was found in the middle of the wall. Officer's note: "The room was still warm. The window had been open for [[two days]]."

Interview Transcript, Excerpt

Leaked from [[District Office ██]]. Authenticity unconfirmed.

FILE 0702 / SUBJECT [[██████]] / SESSION 1 OF 2 / 14:10

DET. [[██████]]: Where were you on the night of the fire?

SUBJECT: At home. I've told you that, but I'm happy to say it again. I made dinner, I read for a while, and I went to bed at about eleven.

DET.: Nobody saw you.

SUBJECT: No. I live alone. I know that makes it harder for you, and I'm sorry for that.

[14:52] Det. [[██████]] raised his voice. Subject remained seated, hands visible on table, tone unchanged.

DET.: (shouting) Stop talking to me like I'm a child! You were there!

SUBJECT: I wasn't. I understand you're under pressure. I didn't do this, and I'll keep telling you so as long as you need to hear it.

[15:20] Det. [[██████]] stood and leaned in to within arm's length of the subject. Subject did not lean back, did not look away, did not blink more than normal. Duty officer later wrote: "He looked at us like a doctor looks at a patient."

DET.: You think this is funny?

SUBJECT: No. I think a lot of people were hurt, and I'm sorry about that. You look tired. Have you eaten today?

[15:41] Session paused. Det. [[██████]] left the room and did not return for the second half.

SESSION 2 OF 2 / DAY 2 / 09:30 / INTERVIEWERS ROTATED

DET. [[██████]]: We have your fingerprints on the doorframe.

SUBJECT: I've been in that building. Many people have. If you tell me what you found, I'll try to help.

[10:05] No fingerprints had been recovered. Detective was testing a bluff. Subject's response: "Then I hope you find what you're looking for."

[11:12] Second interviewer raised his voice and slammed the table. Subject flinched once, apologized for flinching, and resumed the same account word for word.

Detective's closing summary: "Three interviewers. Twelve hours of questioning in total. He never contradicted himself, never got angry, never asked for a lawyer. By the second day, it was the room that was exhausted, not him. I do not think he believed our questions mattered."

FILE 0718 / SUBJECT [[██████]] / SESSION 1 OF 3 / 10:00

DET. [[██████]]: Tell me about the last time you were at the school after hours.

SUBJECT: (quietly) It was a Thursday. I stayed late to grade papers. I'm sorry, I'm just... I keep thinking about those children.

DET.: Which children?

SUBJECT: All of them. Every one of them.

[10:24] Subject began to cry. No sound at first, then a small break in the voice. Tears continued for eleven minutes. Interviewer offered tissues. Subject thanked him and apologized for "making this harder."

DET.: You're crying very easily.

SUBJECT: I've had a lot to carry this year. I'm sorry. I know how it must look.

[10:51] Det. [[██████]] raised his voice and accused the subject of acting. Subject covered her mouth, nodded, and said, "I understand why you'd think that."

SESSION 2 OF 3 / 15:30 / SAME DAY

DET. [[██████]]: (shouting) Stop it! Stop crying! Answer the question!

SUBJECT: I'm trying. I want to help you. I really do.

[15:44] Interviewer stood and moved to within arm's reach. Subject lowered her gaze, her shoulders shook, and she whispered, "Please don't be angry with me." Observing officer behind the glass wrote: "I felt like I was the one who had done something wrong."

[16:10] Subject asked if the interviewer's family was well. Interviewer ended the session.

SESSION 3 OF 3 / DAY 2 / 09:00 / NEW INTERVIEWER

DET. [[██████]]: Your counselor is Dr. [[██████]], correct?

SUBJECT: Yes. She's been wonderful. I couldn't have gotten through this without her.

[09:40] Dr. [[██████]] was contacted by phone. She stated she had never treated the subject, nor met her. Her appointment records contained no such entry.

[09:55] Interviewer confronted the subject with the discrepancy. Subject paused for two seconds, then smiled warmly and said: "Perhaps I misremembered her name. Grief does that."

[10:30] Session ended. Audio of all three sessions was later found blank on playback. Written notes were consistent across all examiners.

Closing note from the final interviewer: "Five hours total. She cried on cue, stopped on cue, and never once forgot to look sad. When I told her she could go, she thanked me and squeezed my hand. I don't know if she was lying. I don't know if I would be able to tell."
